# Space Actuary

A pixel-art web game for the [NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — Space Mission Design Game](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/space-mission-design-game/) challenge, by team Mission Impaysible.

**Play online:** https://dean50312-hub.github.io/space-mission-design-game/

You are the mission designer. Within a fixed budget and your rocket's lift capacity, you choose the orbit, the launch vehicle, and every part of the satellite to maximize its **expected mission value** (mission value × probability of success) — which is exactly what an actuary does. Every number comes from real space engineering: the rocket equation, the diffraction limit, orbital geometry, the inverse-square law for radio signals, and real rockets' flight records.

## What's in the game

- **Five missions:** high-resolution imaging, global climate monitoring, real-time typhoon tracking, broadband communications, and in-situ ionosphere measurements, each modeled on real satellites (WorldView-3, Terra, GOES/Himawari, ViaSat, FORMOSAT-5 and -7). The right orbit isn't a hard-coded rule; it emerges from the physics.
- **Four real rockets:** Electron, PSLV, Falcon 9, and Falcon Heavy, with reliability estimated from their real flight records. A cheaper rocket frees money for better parts at the cost of a higher chance of losing everything.
- **Satellite blueprint and budget sheet:** pick each part directly on the satellite drawing — power, communications, attitude control, structure, flight computer, payload, and propulsion. Cost, mass, power, heat rejection, daily data, reserve, and Δv update live, and every line explains the physics behind it.
- **Trade-offs with no single right answer:** for example, the flight computer comes as a lightweight commercial board, a high-capacity data system, or a radiation-hardened computer. The most expensive one buys "won't break," not processing power.
- **Full mission simulation:** liftoff, a gravity-turn ascent through the atmosphere (boosters, first stage, and fairing separate in order), orbit insertion, and deployment, with each stage's odds shown before the roll. In orbit, the view switches to a real Earth seen from above the North Pole (Natural Earth coastlines), and data only comes down while a ground station can see the satellite.
- **Operations decisions:** part failures and space-weather events drawn from real cases (Kepler K2, Juno, Landsat 7…). You decide whether to spend reserve on a fix or keep flying degraded.
- **Actuarial score:** your design's expected value ÷ the theoretical best under the same conditions (found by exhaustively searching every design). It rates the decision, not the luck, and shows how much each alternative choice would have cost. Each mission also has three challenge medals, plus 13 achievements.
- **Campaign:** four levels gradually introduce propulsion and attitude control, the space environment and the flight computer, and operations decisions. If you get stuck on a level, the mascot ORBIT gives hints.
- **Multiplayer (1–4 players):** each player joins a room from their own device and takes a role — Mission Director, Payload Scientist, Systems Engineer, or Comms & Flight Ops. Everyone edits the same satellite live, but you can only change your own systems. A team chat and an over-budget negotiation panel help the team decide who gives way. Everyone sees the same flight, and a team report shows how each role's choices shaped the result.
- **Custom mode, Space Codex, and quizzes:** pick any mission and event; read full write-ups of every rocket, part, event, and science concept; check the learning goals with a short quiz after each mission.
- Works on desktop and mobile.

## Running locally

No build step or packages needed — serve the project folder with any static web server:

```bash
py -m http.server 5500
```

Then open http://localhost:5500/. Double-clicking `index.html` also works, but some browsers block local music files.

Multiplayer uses Firebase Realtime Database (see [docs/MULTIPLAYER.md](docs/MULTIPLAYER.md)). To try it without Firebase, open http://localhost:5500/?net=local in two tabs of the same browser.

## Project structure

```
index.html          All screens
css/style.css       Visual system (fonts, color tokens, components)
js/                 Game logic (plain JavaScript, no build step)
  data.js           All balance values; sources in docs/REFERENCES.md
  mission.js        Mission model: engineering budgets, mission value, success odds
  operations.js     Mission timeline and Monte Carlo expected value
  scoring.js        Actuarial score and challenge medals
  bestdesigns.js    Theoretical-best design table (generated by a tool)
  multiplayer.js    Rooms, roles, chat, negotiation (net.js is the sync layer)
image/              Pixel art (mostly generated by the scripts in tools/)
audio/bgm/          Background music
tools/              Generators: best designs, pixel icons, satellite blueprints and rockets, flight computers, panel frames, Earth map, launch pad
docs/               Design document, references, asset list, multiplayer setup
```

After changing balance values in `js/data.js`, open `tools/best-designs.html` to recompute the theoretical best designs and paste the output into `js/bestdesigns.js`. If you forget, the game recomputes them on the results screen and warns in the console.
